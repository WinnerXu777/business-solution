# Data and ML plan

Fraud review, sales and demand prediction, and customer profiling are parallel streams. Start data rights, intake, labeling and evaluation in January. Build training and serving for all three streams in staging. For September 2027, sell rules-based fraud review, statistical sales and demand forecasts, and customer profiles grounded in retrieval or structured prompting. With three design partners, plan for zero production fraud models and zero production profile fine-tunes at launch. Activate trained versions per tenant after the data, rights, holdout and shadow checks below pass. Forecasts trained on sufficient imported history follow the same process. [Parallel roadmap](03-roadmap.md)

## Collection, rights and lifecycle

- Ingest purpose-approved data from the tenant's connected systems. Maintain a data dictionary, tenant and source IDs, currency/unit conventions, event time, ingestion time, lineage, label provenance, freshness and correction history. Keep immutable event metadata and separately governed raw payloads.
- Use synthetic order and chargeback sequences, stock-outs, and fictional accounts to build pipelines and test rare failures. Use licence-reviewed public transaction benchmarks for fraud experiments and public retail time series for forecasting comparisons. Use fictional, human-reviewed label examples to test profile prompts and the adapter pipeline. Keep source provenance and use rights. Use public and synthetic data for engineering evidence, with production decisions based on real held-out tenant outcomes.
- Inference on client instructions is the default. Record purpose, data owner, lawful basis, usage rights and access at the dataset level, document vendor training/retention and residency terms, and review upstream connector restrictions. Require a separate written opt-in and applicable legal basis, notices and consent before pooling or training for an additional purpose. Never train shared models on call audio or transcripts. Exclude card data, credentials, raw mailbox content and unnecessary direct identifiers. Pseudonymized data remains governed.
- Isolate training jobs, staging features, vector indexes, evaluation sets and artifacts by tenant and region. Use service roles with a narrow approved purpose and record exports and access. If pooling is approved, create a governed derived feature set for participants only and test re-identification and memorization risk. Define withdrawal, deletion and retraining obligations before launch. Permissioned pooled data can bootstrap shadow fraud or demand candidates and shared-taxonomy research. Hold whole participants out for testing, calibrate against local data and require the local activation evidence below. With three partners, treat pooled results as exploratory.
- Record the dataset snapshot, code and prompt version, model/provider, validation metrics, approvals and expiration in a registry. On tenant exit or a valid deletion request remove eligible source snapshots, derived features, embeddings and tenant artifacts, propagate to providers, and verify. Document time-bounded backup expiry, reapply deletions on restore, and retire or retrain affected models when required. Keep legal holds restricted and traceable. [Privacy and retention](05-governance-security-compliance.md#canadian-data-boundary-and-retention)

## Minimum evidence for trained tenant releases

These are planning floors for each tenant and intended use. Raise them when the error bars, segments or costs warrant it. Counts refer to real, rights-cleared and deduplicated data. Synthetic and public examples can supplement experiments. Require time-ordered holdouts, documented label quality, a prospective shadow run, named customer and ML approval, and rollback to the baseline.

| Stream | Minimum data and evidence before activation |
| --- | --- |
| Fraud review | At least 10,000 eligible orders spanning six months and 200 confirmed, mature fraud outcomes. Reserve the most recent eligible orders for independent testing with at least 50 confirmed positives, leaving at least 150 positives before holdout. Let processor dispute windows mature before labeling. Require at least four weeks of shadow scoring, with outcome follow-up through the label window and an improvement over rules at the same review capacity. |
| Sales and demand forecasting | At least 52 usable weekly observations for a SKU/location series receiving trained output, and 104 for models claiming annual seasonality. Use at least four non-overlapping lead-time backtest windows within the available history and at least four weeks of planner shadow review. Retain seasonal, moving or intermittent-demand baselines for series with sparse or unreliable history and show coverage. |
| Customer profiling fine-tuning | At least 1,000 reviewed examples from distinct tenant accounts under a stable taxonomy. Use at least 800 for development and 200 newer disjoint accounts for independent evaluation, with at least 30 holdout examples per segment enabled. Require at least four weeks of staff shadow review, measurable improvement over grounded prompting and no material quality or privacy regression. Keep current account facts in retrieval. |

## Training and serving stack

Use Python, Polars and scikit-learn for data preparation and baselines, gradient-boosted trees for fraud and candidate forecasts, and PyTorch with PEFT/LoRA for tenant fine-tuning. Version approved training snapshots and artifacts in regional S3 and register evaluations, provenance and promotion in MLflow. Serve batch forecasts and fraud scores through regional workers. Serve language and embedding models through the platform's regional API and use tenant-scoped PostgreSQL/pgvector for retrieval. A premium data, compute, training or inference adapter uses the same approved dataset rights, lineage, deletion and residency checks. [Provider abstraction](01-architecture.md#provider-abstraction)

## Fraud review

| Decision | Plan |
| --- | --- |
| Cold start | Ask tenants and approved processors for timestamped orders, payment/chargeback outcomes, manual review reasons and actual losses. Start with explainable rules for unusual value, account/order velocity and mismatch, using only approved available fields. Route to a queue and sample routine orders for review to reduce label bias. Separate confirmed fraud, suspected, false positives and unresolved disputes. Record outcome maturity and dispute reversals. |
| Model ownership | Start per-tenant rules and calibration. Build the logistic regression and gradient-boosted training and shadow paths concurrently. Fit tenant production candidates where historical outcomes contain enough positives for a useful holdout. A pooled score may help sparse tenants later, with explicit permissions, tenant-separated validation, checks for domain shift and local calibration. Fall back to rules for low volume or missing data. |
| Evaluation | Point-in-time features only. Split by order time, keep related orders and accounts from leaking across datasets, and wait for the label window to mature. Use precision at fixed review capacity, recall and amount-weighted fraud capture, precision-recall AUC, calibration, false positives and review time by channel/account segment. Hold out complete tenants for a pooled model. |
| Release criteria | Pre-register review capacity and the minimum commercially valuable improvement. Compare cost-weighted outcomes to rules at the same queue size, accounting for missed loss and unnecessary reviews. Require stable results across relevant segments and reliable reason codes. If the held-out positives are too sparse to distinguish an improvement, use the native rules and keep collecting labeled outcomes. Shadow first, then use the approved score to prioritize review. Humans retain disposition. |

## Sales and demand forecasting

- **Cold start.** Import historical line items from ERP/QBO and order feeds, plus returns, cancellations, stock availability, known promotions and holidays. Distinguish orders, shipments and actual demand. Flag stock-outs as censored demand and reconcile SKU changes and unit conversions. Use moving or seasonal naive baselines and an intermittent-demand baseline. Where history is short, show aggregate forecasts or abstain at SKU level. A proposed intake target is two years of weekly history for annual seasonality, with shorter-history baselines offered when appropriate.
- **Model ownership.** Start per tenant with shared code and tenant-specific fitted parameters. Fit across related SKUs within one tenant if it helps sparse series. Consider pooling only comparable, permissioned feature series where a leave-tenant-out comparison shows benefit. Forecast weekly at SKU/location and reconcile to totals when hierarchies matter.
- **Evaluation and release.** Backtest with rolling origins at the replenishment lead-time horizon using only known inputs. Compare weighted absolute percentage error where denominator is meaningful, scaled absolute error or absolute units for sparse/zero-volume series, signed bias, interval coverage and stock-out/overstock proxies. A proposed target is at least 10% relative error improvement on agreed segments or a quantified inventory decision benefit, with no material bias regression. Report coverage and abstentions. Run planners in parallel, turn drift or missing feed alerts into baseline fallback, and keep reorder decisions with staff.

## Customer profiling and segmentation

- **Cold start.** Define a small tenant taxonomy with examples from the account owner. Use explicit business signals such as purchase recency, frequency and product mix. Use CRM filters, schema-constrained prompting and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) over authorized tenant records. Require citations to source records, reason codes and abstention when evidence is thin. Staff correct suggestions and supply labeled examples. Do not infer sensitive traits or use segments to establish outreach eligibility, creditworthiness or individualized pricing.
- **Per-tenant default.** Keep taxonomies, curated examples and retrieval indexes tenant-specific. Use the platform-hosted base model or an approved premium route. Build the tenant-specific adapter and managed fine-tuning path in parallel with retrieval. Activate a tenant adapter when examples, rights and measured improvement support it. A pooled model would be a separate proposal with shared taxonomy, permissions, comparable performance across tenants and a workable deletion strategy.
- **Fine-tuning and acceptance.** Tune consistent behavior such as taxonomy and output discipline. Quantify the improvement over prompted and retrieval-based output. Keep changing facts in retrieval. Apply the minimum evidence for trained tenant releases above. De-duplicate by account, separate older training examples from newer holdout accounts and compare rules, prompting, RAG and fine-tuning under identical test conditions. Agree a lift threshold before training, for example five macro-F1 points or enough reduced review time to repay training and operating cost over a quarter. Require no regression in rare-segment quality, groundedness, leakage or sensitive inferences. Provider access boundaries, output extraction tests, portability and artifact deletion must pass before production.
- **Production monitoring.** Track precision/recall by segment, abstentions, citation accuracy, staff overrides, latency and cost per reviewed profile. Check retrieval access on every request. Keep the simpler baseline or route to a human when the model drifts or the provider changes unexpectedly.

## Shared release process

Build a versioned evaluation suite for prompts, models, policies and tool executors. Include cross-tenant retrieval, prompt injection, stale data, unauthorized tools, repeated events, missing values and manual overrides. Run offline evaluation and replay without external effects, then shadow on permitted inputs, activate for a supervised eligible tenant and audit changes before widening. A confidentiality failure, unauthorized action or untraceable material decision blocks the affected release. Validate synthetic fixtures and representative licensed benchmarks even when the first customer lacks enough outcomes. Keep tenant-scoped rollback, baseline fallback and a log of who approved each release. Set alerts on feed lag, drift, cost and override rates by tenant. Maintain an owner and review date for each active model.
