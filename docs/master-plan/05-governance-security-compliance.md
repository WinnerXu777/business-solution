# Governance, security and compliance

Launch in Toronto, Ontario. Canadian privacy, electronic messaging, tax records and Ontario collections controls are built alongside the product. Counsel reviews the actual operating model, customer contracts, provider flows and templates during development. Record signoffs by jurisdiction and capability. [Parallel roadmap](03-roadmap.md)

## Phase 0 employment conflict and IP

The founder's full-time work building a government AI platform creates a risk of overlapping duties, employer ownership claims and misuse of confidential material. Make this review a Phase 0 prerequisite for staffed delivery and for any reuse of code, designs, prompts or patterns. Review the employment contract, IP and invention assignment terms, confidentiality obligations and the employer's conflict-of-interest and outside-activity policies. Check which Ontario public-sector rules apply to the actual role. For example, section 8 of Ontario Regulation 381/07 addresses outside employment and undertakings for ministry public servants. Follow the employer's disclosure process and obtain written clearance from the proper employer or ethics authority if required. Arrange a short Ontario employment-lawyer review of the documents, proposed scope and IP boundaries. [Ontario Regulation 381/07](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/r07381)

Build on personal time and personal equipment using separate accounts, repositories, tools and credentials. Keep government data, employer materials and confidential information out of the platform, training, prompts and AI development tools. Record decisions and the provenance and permissions for any proposed reuse. Use independently developed work and approved public sources when rights are unclear. Hold the affected activity if clearance or ownership is unresolved. Record asset assignments from contractors and hires. [Phase 0 decision and risk](07-open-questions.md#phase-0-employment-review)

| Policy settings | Platform restrictions take precedence. Tenants can narrow tools, fields, recipients, hours, budget and approval triggers. ## Security and operations

- Enforce least privilege on identity, databases, queues, storage, backups, retrieval, training and connectors. Verify tenant scope at each boundary. Use encryption in transit and at rest with managed keys. Rotate secrets, check webhook signatures and replay windows, separate production access and keep tenant data out of development.
- Make break-glass access time-limited, ticketed, logged and independently reviewed. Minimize sensitive fields in model inputs and telemetry. Announce AI use and offer human handoff. Use account-bound verification before disclosing a customer's order or account details. Default to live speech processing without durable audio or full transcripts. Review purpose, notice, consent and geography for any recording.
- Review subprocessors, track dependency vulnerabilities, require deployment review and test isolation and restores. Keep regional backups with separate permissions. Initial targets are an RPO of up to 24 hours of data and an RTO of up to one business day. Confirm measured performance before promising service levels.
- Name an incident commander and backup, exercise credential revocation and pause procedures, and preserve evidence. Maintain customer notification paths and legal clocks. The second operator runs a restore and containment drill before live service. [Staffing and ownership](03-roadmap.md#staffing-and-operating-rhythm)

## Outbound voice and SMS collections gate

Build outbound AI voice and SMS in parallel from January. Keep live dispatch disabled by default for each tenant, provider and channel. Start with approved creditor-operated outreach about existing Ontario corporate B2B invoices. Verify debtor incorporation, business purpose, no personal guarantee, contact authority, jurisdiction and current balance. Route consumer debts, sole proprietors, personally guaranteed, disputed, represented, bankrupt and unclassified accounts to creditor staff. Keep collections scripts free of marketing. Use bounded reminders, verified identity before any debt disclosure, and human handoff for disputes, negotiation and payment arrangements. Skip voicemail at first and keep texts free of debt details pending verification.

| Review area | Launch controls |
| --- | --- |
| CRTC rules and National DNCL | The CRTC treats calls made solely for account collection without solicitation as outside telemarketing. National Do Not Call List rules are for telemarketing and exempt calls to business consumers. That business exemption does not remove otherwise applicable Telemarketing or ADAD obligations. Have counsel classify the exact scripts, recipients and route. If any future outreach is telemarketing, separately review registration, subscription and list screening where required, internal do-not-call, and prior express consent specific to the caller and number for ADAD solicitation. Keep solicitation out of collections. [CRTC Unsolicited Telecommunications Rules, Parts II–V](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/trules-reglest.htm) · [CRTC debt collection decision](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/archive/2015/2015-117.htm) |
| ADAD and AI voice | Design to the CRTC automatic dialing-announcing device (ADAD) controls for non-solicitation calls. Have counsel approve how the greeting identifies the creditor, provides a brief privacy-safe purpose and supplies a local or toll-free callback number and an email or mailing address. Disclose AI, verify caller ID and callback handling, repeat the identification where required on calls over 60 seconds, test prompt disconnect on hang-up, and prohibit sequential dialing and protected destinations. Apply recipient-local federal hours or applicable provincial hours under Part IV. The CRTC's 2026-132 review asks how the rules should treat synthesized and AI voices and non-solicitation calls. Comments and replies have closed. Track the pending outcome and recheck the docket, current rules and effective dates before any live pilot and wider release. [CRTC Part IV](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/trules-reglest.htm) · [CRTC consultation 2026-132](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/archive/2026/2026-132.htm) |
| CASL and SMS | Classify each text against CASL's commercial electronic message test. Have counsel document whether an exception applies, including a consent-only exception under section 6(6) or the narrow legal-right enforcement exemption under regulation section 3(c), if justified for the exact message. For applicable messages require the correct consent or other basis, identification and working unsubscribe. As product policy, require separate opt-in for collections SMS, include creditor identification and support STOP and equivalent requests. Keep marketing and win-back permissions separate. [CRTC CASL guidance](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm/) · [CASL section 6](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/section-6.html) · [Electronic Commerce Protection Regulations section 3](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2013-221/section-3.html) |
| Ontario CDSSA and contact limits | Ontario counsel confirms the creditor/platform roles, registration, corporate debtor exemption under Regulation 74 section 19.7 and implications where the debtor or guarantor is an individual. For covered agency collections, sections 21–22 include initial written notice and a six-day wait subject to exceptions, call hours generally 7 a.m.–9 p.m. Monday–Saturday and 1–5 p.m. Sunday in the recipient's local time, no calls on listed holidays absent applicable exceptions, and a general cap of three contacts in seven days on behalf of the same creditor after speaking with the debtor, subject to exceptions. For the initial B2B product apply a stricter 9 a.m.–5 p.m. weekday window for all channels, block listed holidays, require any applicable notice and waiting period, and cap at three attempts across email, voice and SMS in a rolling seven days per creditor and debtor. Include logged manual contacts and stop if the contact history is incomplete. Counsel reviews harassment, debtor charges and any stricter limits before release. [Ontario CDSSA](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c14) · [Ontario Regulation 74](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/900074) · [Ontario prohibited practices guide](https://www.ontario.ca/document/guide-collection-agencies/prohibited-practices-and-conduct) |
| Call recording and privacy | Default to live processing without durable audio or full transcripts. If counsel approves recording for a specific purpose, tell the recipient at the start, obtain and log express recording consent before recording, offer an unrecorded route, and restrict access, use and retention. Keep consent to record separate from consent to receive AI calls. Keep debt information private until verification. [OPC recording guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/surveillance/02_05_d_14/) |
| Consent, opt-out and shutdown | Capture express opt-in by channel and verified number for this launch, including AI voice, named creditor, purpose, consent text and version, source, timestamp, expiry and revocation. Accept SMS STOP, spoken do-not-call requests and staff-entered requests. Suppress immediately across applicable routes and synchronize with CRM, carrier and queued jobs. Recheck on every dispatch and fail closed when consent, contact history or suppression cannot be verified. Give tenant and platform operators kill switches by tenant, provider and channel, including queue cancellation, active call termination and stop verification with the carrier. Use governed human follow-up after review. |

**Live launch condition.** Ontario counsel signs off the exact debtor classes, scripts, rules and sender model. The delivery lead and security reviewer approve carrier readiness, privacy and test evidence. The tenant signs the scope, supplies verified opt-ins and owns replies, holds and disputes. Rehearse opt-out, wrong party, revoked approval, stale balance, timed contact caps, unknown delivery and the kill switch on both native and approved BYO routes. Start with one supervised tenant and a volume cap, review every outcome during the pilot and widen by channel only after documented review. Keep the corresponding channel disabled until its evidence is complete. [Parallel calendar](03-roadmap.md)

## Canadian data boundary and retention

Keep tenant databases, objects, audit, retrieval, training and backup/recovery in Canada with AWS Canada Central as the launch region. Document processing, storage, retention, support access and training use for each mail, telephony, payment, identity and model provider, including customer-selected accounts. Assess third-party routes before use and record any approved transfers in the tenant's configuration and contracts. Check residency and purpose on each route and fallback. [Provider routing controls](01-architecture.md#provider-abstraction)

Define retention by data class and processing purpose. Keep governed accounting evidence and holds accessible and restrict its use. Let authorized staff export business and audit records in structured, readable format. Time-limit operational payloads and raw evidence by policy. Record traceable amendments, delete eligible records and associated features/embeddings/artifacts, propagate requests to subprocessors and reapply valid deletions after a restore. Define contract export windows and confirm deletion at exit. [Data and ML lifecycle](04-data-ml-plan.md#collection-rights-and-lifecycle)

## Geographic expansion

**US second.** Select the first state using demand, partner coverage and counsel review. Review state licensing and first-party commercial collections rules, federal and state debt collection exposure, CAN-SPAM, recording and privacy, tax records, SaaS tax and regional data/incident obligations. Keep outbound voice/SMS off until counsel reviews TCPA applicability by number type and dialing technology, prior express consent and revocation, any exemption, state-specific calling and recording rules, and applicable federal and state debt collection limits. FCC 24-17 treats AI-generated voices as artificial voices under the TCPA. Apply the corresponding consent, identification and opt-out requirements, including prior express written consent for covered telemarketing calls. Review robotext and reassigned-number controls separately. Enable only signed-off states and templates. [CFPB Regulation F](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/rules-policy/regulations/1006/) · [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business) · [FCC AI voice ruling](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-17A1.pdf)

**Quebec separately.** Commission a Law 25 review before any Quebec rollout. Evaluate relevant system privacy impact assessments and transfers outside Quebec, including backup and support paths, plus written agreements, portability and automated-decision duties where applicable. Confirm applicable customer-facing language and provincial obligations with Quebec counsel. [Quebec privacy regulator](https://www.cai.gouv.qc.ca/protection-renseignements-personnels/sujets-et-domaines-dinteret/principaux-changements-loi-25)

## Contracts and assurance

Use an MSA, DPA, security annex, acceptable-use terms, module order forms and collections schedule. Allocate responsibility for classification, consent, balances and configuration to the customer where applicable. Allocate controls, own processing, subprocessors and incident response to the platform. Specify region and provider choices, purpose, issue escalation, portability and exit. Confirm tech E&O and cyber coverage for communications, collections, AI and data processing with the broker.

Start SOC 2 control ownership and evidence in January. Scope the engagement with an independent CPA by June. Start with Security and assess Availability, Confidentiality and ERP Processing Integrity with the auditor. Budget readiness, independent testing and Type II work. Use Type I if a buyer or auditor calls for it within budget. Agree an observation period after controls operate consistently and document actual scope and dates. Audit timing depends on control maturity and the auditor's schedule.
