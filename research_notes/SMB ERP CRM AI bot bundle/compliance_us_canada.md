## 1. Voice bots and chatbots: short summary of calling, recording and AI-disclosure rules

### Takeaway
The main rules for outbound AI voice calls and texts, call recording, and bot disclosure are summarized below. In short: get consent before outbound AI calls and texts, honor opt-outs fast, announce recording and AI status at the start of every call, and keep the vendor from making secondary use of call data.

**United States**
- **AI voice is "artificial voice" under the TCPA.** The FCC's February 2024 Declaratory Ruling (FCC 24-17) says so. Outbound AI-voice calls and texts to cell phones need prior express consent, and prior express *written* consent if the content is marketing. This applies to business-owned cell phones too.
- **Damages** are $500–$1,500 per call.
- **The one-to-one consent rule was vacated** by the 11th Circuit on Jan 24, 2025.
- **FCC interpretations no longer bind courts** after *McLaughlin v. McKesson* (U.S. June 2025).
- **Revocation of consent:**
  - Since April 11, 2025, consumers can revoke by any reasonable means, and callers have up to 10 business days to honor it.
  - An order adopted Sept 30, 2026 (released Oct 1, 2026) replaced the "revoke-all" rule. Opt-outs from informational messages now apply only to that category, and callers may designate exclusive opt-out methods.
  - The new order takes effect 30 days after Federal Register publication. That is reported as Nov 25, 2026 but not confirmed here.
  - **PENDING:** an FNPRM on a shorter revocation window and a mandatory "revoke-all" option.
- **Carrier and caller-ID rules:** SMS needs A2P 10DLC brand and campaign registration (a carrier rule, not a statute). STIR/SHAKEN applies to voice. Florida, Oklahoma and other states have their own mini-TCPAs for sales calls.
- **Recording:**
  - Design for all-party consent: California, Florida, Illinois, Washington and others require it.
  - California's CIPA ($5,000 per violation) is the main litigation risk for AI call vendors.
  - Courts in *Taylor v. ConverseNow* and the Invoca case (motion to dismiss denied Nov 2025) let claims proceed against AI vendors as possible third-party "eavesdroppers," where the vendor could use call data for its own purposes.
- **AI-disclosure laws:**
  - California SB 1001 covers online bots.
  - Utah's AI Policy Act requires disclosure on request, and proactive disclosure for regulated occupations.
  - Maine LD 1727 requires clear and conspicuous disclosure in trade.
  - About 15 states have chatbot laws, mostly aimed at companion and mental-health bots.
  - Colorado's AI Act was repealed and replaced by SB 26-189 (signed May 14, 2026, effective Jan 1, 2027; narrower and notice-based).

**Canada**
- The CRTC's Unsolicited Telecommunications Rules apply. Automated (ADAD) telemarketing needs express consent.
- **PENDING:** CRTC consultation 2026-132 (June 11, 2026) on whether the ADAD definition covers AI voices.

**Enforcement examples:** FCC $6M forfeiture against Kramer and a $1M Lingo Telecom settlement over AI-cloned robocalls (2024). *Lowrey v. OpenAI & Twilio* (Dec 2025) tests platform liability for user-sent AI texts.

### Inferences
- **The ERP/CRM tie-in matters.** If CRM records feed bot outreach, the CRM must store per-contact data showing whether outreach is allowed:
  - consent source, timestamp, scope and wording
  - opt-outs
  - consumer vs business flag
  - province or state
  
  Without this, the bot features cannot be operated compliantly.

## 2. Privacy law for a vendor hosting SMB clients' ERP/CRM data (US and Canada): which role, which obligations

### Takeaway
In every regime, the SMB client is the controller (the "business" or accountable "organization"). The vendor is a service provider or processor. The vendor's main legal obligations come through **contracts it is legally required to sign** (CCPA service-provider terms, state processor terms, PIPEDA accountability terms, Quebec Law 25 written agreements). Separately, the vendor is directly liable for:
- its own security failures (FTC Act § 5),
- breach notice to clients, and
- any secondary use of client data.

Quebec Law 25 is the strictest regime in either country. An ERP/CRM must support its data-portability, retention and automated-decision features.

### Cited Findings
**United States**
- **CCPA/CPRA "business" thresholds:** revenue over $25M (CPI-adjusted to about $26.6M), 100k or more consumers or households, or 50% or more of revenue from selling or sharing personal information. Most SMB clients fall below these. — [Cal. Civ. Code 1798.140](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.140) [P]
  - The vendor counts as a "business" only for its *own* data, such as its own customers and marketing.
  - B2B contact data (employee and contact names in a CRM) has been fully in scope since Jan 1, 2023.
- **Service-provider contract terms the vendor must accept when a client *is* covered** [P] — [CPPA regulations](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/):
  - process only for specified business purposes
  - no selling, sharing, or combining with other data
  - flow-down to sub-processors
  - assist with consumer requests
  - client audit rights
  - notify the client if the vendor can no longer comply
  
  Legal basis: Civ. Code § 1798.100(d) and § 1798.140(ag); 11 CCR § 7051.
- **CPPA ADMT, risk-assessment and cybersecurity-audit regulations:** adopted 2025. ADMT duties start Jan 1, 2027, and audits are phased 2028–2030. These are relevant if clients use the platform for "significant decisions" about individuals, such as credit holds or employment decisions. — [CPPA](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/) [P; verify dates]
- **Other state comprehensive privacy laws:** about 20 states, including Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Texas and Oregon. [P]
  - Most apply at roughly 100k consumers, so small clients are usually exempt.
  - Texas and Nebraska tie applicability to not being a "small business" under SBA definitions.
  - All require controller–processor contracts with instructions, confidentiality, deletion or return, sub-processor notice, and audit cooperation.
- **FTC Act § 5:** unreasonable data security and misrepresenting security are unfair or deceptive practices. — [FTC data security](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/privacy-security/data-security) [P]
- **Directly analogous enforcement: Blackbaud.** Blackbaud is a cloud CRM/fundraising SaaS whose 2020 ransomware breach exposed its clients' donor data. [P; verify figures]
  - FTC consent order, 2024: data-retention limits, deletion of unnecessary data, and a security program.
  - SEC settlement, 2023: $3M, for misleading disclosures.
  - Multistate attorney-general settlement, Oct 2023: $49.5M.
  
  Source: [FTC Blackbaud](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/blackbaud-inc)
- **Breach notification:** all 50 states have statutes. A vendor that "maintains" data it doesn't own must notify the data owner (the client), for example under Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.82(b). The client then notifies individuals and regulators. — [Cal. Civ. Code 1798.82](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.82) [P]
- **GLBA Safeguards Rule (16 C.F.R. Part 314):** applies to clients that are non-bank "financial institutions," such as businesses extending consumer credit, tax preparers and auto dealers. — [16 CFR 314](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-314) [P]
  - Requires the client to oversee service providers by contract.
  - Encryption and MFA are required.
  - Breaches affecting 500 or more consumers must be reported to the FTC within 30 days (since May 13, 2024).
  - Applies to consumer customers only, not B2B.

**Canada**
- **PIPEDA accountability principle (Sched. 1, 4.1.3):** the client stays accountable for personal information sent to a processor and must ensure "comparable protection" by contract. — [PIPEDA](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/P-8.6/) [P]
- **Breach reporting:** breaches posing a "real risk of significant harm" go to the OPC and affected individuals, and breach records are kept 24 months. [P]
- **PIPEDA fines** are capped at C$100,000 per offence. — [DataGrail](https://www.datagrail.io/glossary/quebec-bill-64/) [V]
- **Federal reform** (Bill C-27, the CPPA and AIDA) died in January 2025. No successor was verified this session. [P]
- **Provincial private-sector laws:** Alberta PIPA, BC PIPA and Quebec's private-sector Act replace PIPEDA for in-province commercial activity. [P]
- **Quebec Law 25** (private-sector Act, CQLR c. P-39.1, as amended 2022–2024) [P unless noted] — [LégisQuébec P-39.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/P-39.1):
  - The person with the highest authority is the default privacy officer, and their contact details must be published.
  - A PIA is required for any information-system project involving personal information.
  - Organizations must keep a confidentiality-incident register and report serious incidents to the CAI.
  - Notice is required of decisions made *exclusively* by automated processing, with a right to human review (s. 12.1).
  - The most privacy-protective settings must be the default.
  - Retention must be limited, followed by destruction or anonymization.
  - **Data portability (since Sept 22, 2024):** individuals can obtain their computerized personal information in a "structured, commonly used technological format."
  - Penalties (verified this session): administrative monetary penalties up to C$10M or 2% of worldwide turnover; penal fines up to C$25M or 4%. A private right of action is available, with punitive damages for intentional or grossly negligent breaches. — [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2023/09/law-25-quebec-s-second-wave-of-new-privacy-amendme); [DataGrail](https://www.datagrail.io/glossary/quebec-bill-64/) [V]

### Inferences
- **ERP/CRM features that make clients compliant** (selling points as well as obligations):
  - per-record purpose and retention policies with automated deletion
  - export of a person's data in JSON or CSV (Quebec portability; CCPA access requests)
  - a deletion workflow that respects accounting-record retention (see section 5)
  - field-level tagging of sensitive data
  - an audit log of who accessed what
  - consent and contact-preference objects
  - an "automated decision" flag with a human-review queue (Quebec s. 12.1; future CCPA ADMT)
- **The vendor's own exposure is driven by two things:**
  1. Secondary use of client data, such as training shared AI models, analytics, or cross-client benchmarking. Prohibited without a contractual basis, and it can make the vendor a "third party" or "business" rather than a service provider.
  2. Security failures (the Blackbaud pattern).

### Gaps
- Exact 2026 CPI-adjusted CCPA thresholds and penalties.
- Whether a federal Canadian privacy bill was reintroduced in 2025–2026.
- Text of the Quebec portability regulation.

## 3. Cross-border data residency (US-hosted data for Canadian clients, and vice versa)

### Takeaway
Canadian **private-sector** privacy law has **no general data-localization rule**. PIPEDA, BC PIPA and Alberta PIPA allow US hosting with notice and contractual safeguards. Three exceptions matter:
1. **Quebec Law 25 s. 17** requires the *client* to do a transfer PIA before personal information leaves Quebec, including to other provinces and the US.
2. **The CRA expects tax records to be kept in Canada.** Records on foreign servers need written CRA permission (see section 5).
3. **Public-sector and health customers, and RFPs,** often contractually require Canadian residency.

US law has no general residency requirement for SMB data.

### Cited Findings
- **Private sector:** under PIPEDA there is no general restriction on transferring personal information outside Canada, and BC PIPA does not restrict storage or access outside Canada. Private-sector contracts and RFPs still frequently require Canadian residency. — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/canadian-data-residency-requirements-a-few-more-thoughts-on-a-tricky-subject); [IAPP, US cloud services](https://iapp.org/news/a/canadians-and-us-cloud-services-does-privacy-compliance-translate-across-borders) [V]
- **OPC 2009 cross-border processing guidelines:** organizations should tell individuals their information may be processed abroad and be accessible to foreign authorities, and should protect it by contract. — [OPC guidelines](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/airports-and-borders/gl_dab_090127/) [P]
- **BC public bodies:** BC's 2021 FIPPA amendments (Bill 22, mostly in force Nov 25, 2021) removed strict residency. A PIA is now required before sensitive personal information is disclosed for storage outside Canada. — [BLG](https://www.blg.com/-/media/insights/documents/changes-to-bcs-public-sector-privacy-legislation-jan2022.pdf); [UBC](https://privacymatters.ubc.ca/resources/bill22); [BC gov guidance](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/services-for-government/information-management-technology/privacy/privacy-impact-assessments/guidance-on-disclosures-outside-of-canada) [V]
- **Nova Scotia public bodies:** PIIDPA generally prohibits storage or access outside Canada, with exceptions the head of the public body can authorize. — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/canadian-data-residency-requirements-a-few-more-thoughts-on-a-tricky-subject) [V]
- **Quebec Law 25 transfer PIA:**
  - The PIA must weigh the data's sensitivity, the purpose of the transfer, the safeguards in place, and the legal framework of the destination.
  - The transfer may proceed only if the information would receive adequate protection, and it must be governed by a written agreement.
  - Companies outside Quebec have described s. 17 as a significant burden.
  
  Sources: [IAPP](https://www.iapp.org/news/a/iapp-cps-23-forthcoming-quebec-privacy-regulations-situate-province-between-canada-and-europe); [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en-ca/topics/canadian-privacy-laws-new-rules-for-a-new-era/quebec-law-25) [V]
- **Federal government cloud:** the Government of Canada's data sovereignty white paper discusses foreign lawful-access risk (for example, the US CLOUD Act) for public cloud. This is relevant to vendor sales into the public sector or regulated sectors. — [GC White Paper](https://www.canada.ca/en/government/system/digital-government/digital-government-innovations/cloud-services/gc-white-paper-data-sovereignty-public-cloud.html) [V]
- **Health data:** some provincial health-information laws (Ontario PHIPA, Alberta HIA) and health-sector policies impose added controls or residency expectations. — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/canadian-data-residency-requirements-a-few-more-thoughts-on-a-tricky-subject) [V, general]

### Inferences
- **Offer a Canadian region** (for example, AWS ca-central-1 or Azure Canada Central) as a tier. It solves CRA record-location issues, Quebec PIA friction and RFP requirements in one move.
- **Publish a "Quebec transfer PIA support pack":**
  - data-flow map
  - hosting and sub-processor locations
  - encryption at rest and in transit
  - key management
  - access controls and support-staff locations
  - lawful-access request policy
  - breach-notice SLA
- **Remote support counts as "access."** Support staff in other countries accessing data is itself a cross-border access, so disclose it.

### Gaps
- Whether Quebec revised the s. 17 "adequate protection" standard.
- Current Nova Scotia PIIDPA text.
- Any 2025–2026 federal sovereignty-cloud procurement rules.

## 4. Security assurance expectations: SOC 2 (and SOC 1), ISO 27001

### Takeaway
SOC 2 is **not legally required**, but it is commercially expected of SaaS vendors hosting business data. Type II is the norm buyers ask for. It also serves as the vendor's evidence of "reasonable security" under the FTC Act, the GLBA service-provider oversight rules, the HIPAA Security Rule and PIPEDA safeguards. An ERP that holds clients' general ledger and financial reporting may also be asked for a **SOC 1** report by clients' outside auditors.

### Cited Findings
- SOC 2 is not a legal requirement for SaaS companies, but it is "commercially mandatory" in practice. Type II is widely expected in vendor security assessments. — [Bright Defense](https://www.brightdefense.com/resources/soc-2-for-saas/); [Sprinto](https://sprinto.com/soc-2/type-2/) [V; vendor sources]
- **Common path:** Type I first, then a 3–12 month observation window, then the Type II audit (about 4–6 weeks). — [Sprinto](https://sprinto.com/soc-2/type-2/) [V]
- **Cost estimates vary widely:** about $40k–$80k for a first-time Type II at a 10–50 person SaaS (consultant estimate), versus $14k–$23k for small AWS-hosted scopes (another estimate). — [Hudson Valley consultant blog](https://sm911.github.io/compliance-blog/aicpa/soc2-type2-for-saas-smbs.html); [soc2auditors.org](https://soc2auditors.org/insights/soc-2-audit-for-small-business/) [V; low-authority sources]
- **Trust Services Criteria:** security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality and privacy. Only security is required. A revision touching change management is expected around 2027. — [Accounting Today](https://www.accountingtoday.com/opinion/soc-2-is-in-our-dna-why-the-2027-trust-services-criteria-matter) [V]
- **SOC 1 (SSAE 18)** reports on controls relevant to user entities' internal control over financial reporting. Auditors of ERP-dependent clients rely on it. — [AICPA SOC suite](https://www.aicpa-cima.com/topic/audit-assurance/audit-and-assurance-greater-than-soc-2) [P]

### Inferences
- **Priority order for a small vendor:**
  1. Security baseline: MFA everywhere, encryption, logging, backups with restore tests, vulnerability management, and vendor management of the LLM, telephony and cloud sub-processors.
  2. SOC 2 Type I.
  3. SOC 2 Type II, scoped to security, availability and confidentiality, adding processing integrity for the ERP.
  4. Optionally a SOC 1 when clients' auditors start asking.
- **Include the AI pipeline in scope.** Prompt and transcript logs, model-provider data retention, and tenant isolation in retrieval (RAG) indexes are now standard security-review questions. [Inference]

## 5. Financial and accounting data obligations: record retention, audit trails, sales tax and GST/HST

### Takeaway
The **client** carries the tax record-keeping duty, and using a third party "does not relieve the taxpayer" of it. The vendor must therefore build features that let clients comply:
- immutable or append-only audit trails
- retention locks that override "delete" requests
- export in readable formats for years after cancellation
- (for Canadian clients) Canadian storage, or guidance on getting CRA permission

The vendor's own sales-tax registration and collection on its SaaS fees is a separate issue (US economic nexus; Canadian GST/HST and QST).

### Cited Findings
**US record-keeping**
- IRC § 6001 requires taxpayers to keep records sufficient to establish tax liability. — [26 USC 6001](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6001) [P]
- IRS guidance on retention periods: generally 3 years after filing; 6 years if income was underreported by more than 25%; 7 years for bad-debt or worthless-securities claims; at least 4 years for employment tax records. — [IRS: How long should I keep records](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-long-should-i-keep-records) [P]
- **Rev. Proc. 98-25 (electronic records):**
  - Machine-sensible records must be retained "so long as their contents may become material." They must be available to the IRS on request and capable of being retrieved, processed, printed and produced.
  - Using a third party for storage "does not relieve" the taxpayer of record-keeping obligations.
  - It mandatorily applies to taxpayers with $10M or more in assets; others are under the general rule. The writer should verify the precise scope language.
  
  Sources: [Rev. Proc. 98-25](https://www.eitc.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-98-25.pdf); [IRS automated records](https://www.irs.gov/node/4437) [V]
- Rev. Proc. 98-25 also expects an audit trail between the general ledger and source documents, and documentation of the system. — [Rev. Proc. 98-25](https://www.eitc.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/rp-98-25.pdf) [P; not confirmed in fetched snippet]
- Sarbanes-Oxley internal-control rules apply to public companies, not to private SMB clients. [P]

**Canada record-keeping**
- **Six-year retention:** under the Income Tax Act s. 230 and the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST) s. 286, records must be kept for six years from the end of the last year they relate to. Electronic records must stay in an electronically readable format. Longer periods apply for objections, appeals and loss carryforwards. — [CRA: Where to keep records / how long](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records/where-keep-your-records-long-request-permission-destroy-them-early.html); [CRA IC05-1 Electronic Record Keeping](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/ic05-1/electronic-record-keeping.html); [CRA GST/HST memo 15-2](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/15-2/computerized-records-revised-june-2005.html) [V]
- **CRA's position on location:** records kept outside Canada and accessed electronically from Canada are not considered "kept in Canada." Written permission from the CRA is needed to keep them abroad, and the records must be available to CRA auditors at all reasonable times. Registered charities cannot get this permission. — [CRA](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records/where-keep-your-records-long-request-permission-destroy-them-early.html); [Charity Law Group](https://charitylawgroup.ca/charity-law-questions/how-can-canadian-charities-ensure-their-cloud-storage-is-compliant) [V]
- **Possible tension with eBay Canada:** a 2008 Federal Court of Appeal decision (*eBay Canada*) suggested data accessible from Canada can be treated as located in Canada, but CRA policy has not changed. — [Charity Law Group](https://charitylawgroup.ca/charity-law-questions/how-can-canadian-charities-ensure-their-cloud-storage-is-compliant) [V]
- **CRA IC05-1 expectations:** electronic systems should keep audit trails, back-ups and system documentation. — [CRA IC05-1](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/ic05-1/electronic-record-keeping.html) [P on the specific audit-trail wording]

**Sales tax and GST/HST: client-facing functions**
- **US:** after *South Dakota v. Wayfair* (2018), states impose economic-nexus collection duties, commonly at $100k in sales. Taxability of SaaS and digital services varies by state. — [Wayfair opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/17pdf/17-494_j4el.pdf) [P]
  - The client is liable for collecting and remitting.
  - An ERP's tax engine (built in, or third-party such as Avalara) is a contract-risk item: who pays if a tax calculation is wrong.
- **Canada:** clients registered for GST/HST and QST must show the required invoice information. ITC documentation requirements are set by regulation and vary by invoice amount. — [CRA GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses.html) [P]
  - Quebec has sector-specific mandatory billing modules (the SRM for restaurants and bars). This is relevant if the vendor targets that niche. [P]

**The vendor's own SaaS fees**
- **Canada's simplified GST/HST regime** (since July 1, 2021): non-resident vendors with more than C$30,000 in taxable digital supplies to Canadian *consumers* over 12 months must register and collect. B2B sales are generally handled by the business customer through self-assessment. Simplified registrants cannot claim input tax credits. — [CRA digital economy](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/digital-economy.html); [Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/blog/en/north-america/2021/03/canada-to-tax-non-resident-sales-of-digital-products-and-more.html) [V]
- A Canadian-resident vendor (or a non-resident carrying on business in Canada) registers normally once it exceeds the C$30,000 small-supplier threshold. [P]
- Quebec has required certain non-resident digital suppliers to register for QST since 2019. — [CPA Canada](https://www.cpacanada.ca/business-and-accounting-resources/taxation/blog/2021/february/gst-hst-proposals-for-digital-sales) [V, general]
- US states that tax SaaS may require the vendor to collect sales tax on subscription fees once nexus thresholds are met. [P]

### Inferences
**ERP design requirements**
- Posted journal entries are immutable. Corrections are made by reversing entries.
- The audit log records user, timestamp, before and after values, and source document link.
- Retention locks run 6 or more years for Canadian clients and 7 or more years as the US default, and they override privacy "delete" requests. The data is archived or restricted rather than destroyed, and the reason is documented.
- An "export everything" function produces non-proprietary formats (CSV, PDF, XML).
- After termination, the client has a data-export window plus an optional paid archival tier.
- Canadian data residency is available. Otherwise, the vendor documents how clients can seek CRA permission.

**Contract terms**
- The client is responsible for its tax determinations and filings.
- The vendor warrants that the platform calculates correctly according to the configuration, and caps that liability.

### Gaps
- Full text of Rev. Proc. 98-25 audit-trail provisions.
- Revenu Québec record-location rules (Taxation Act / QST Act) were not checked.
- State-by-state SaaS taxability was not researched.

## 6. CRM email and SMS marketing: CAN-SPAM (US) and CASL (Canada)

### Takeaway
- **CAN-SPAM is an opt-out regime.** It also covers B2B emails, with penalties up to $53,088 per email.
- **CASL is an opt-in regime.** Consent is required before sending. It covers B2B, with only narrow exemptions, and penalties up to C$10M per violation.
- **A CRM vendor that sends on clients' behalf** can be pulled in: under CASL for "causing or permitting" a send or "aiding" a violation, and under CAN-SPAM only if it "initiates" the message.

Build consent records, unsubscribe handling and sender identification into the CRM's email and SMS modules.

### Cited Findings
**CAN-SPAM** (15 U.S.C. § 7701 et seq.; 16 C.F.R. Part 316) [P] — [FTC CAN-SPAM guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business)
- Applies to all commercial email, including B2B.
- Requires:
  - accurate header and "from" information
  - non-deceptive subject lines
  - identification of the message as an ad, unless the recipient opted in
  - a valid physical postal address
  - a working opt-out that is honored within 10 business days
- Opt-out addresses may not be sold.
- Both the advertiser and the "initiator" are liable.
- Transactional or relationship messages are largely exempt, but must not contain false routing information.
- **Penalty:** maximum civil penalty of **$53,088 per violating email**, effective Jan 17, 2025. The FTC made no 2026 adjustment. — [Federal Register 2025-01361](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/17/2025-01361/adjustments-to-civil-penalty-amounts); [FTC 2026 notice](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-18853.pdf) [V]
- **Largest CAN-SPAM-related penalty:** Verkada, $2.95M (2024). — [Prospeo summary](https://prospeo.io/s/can-spam-penalties) [V; secondary source, verify on ftc.gov]
- **Mailbox-provider rules (contractual, not legal):** Gmail and Yahoo have required these from bulk senders since February 2024:
  - SPF, DKIM and DMARC authentication
  - one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058)
  - spam-complaint rate kept below 0.3%
  
  Source: [Google sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126) [P]

**CASL** (S.C. 2010, c. 23) [P] — [CASL](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/E-1.6/); [CRTC CASL guidance](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm)
- Commercial electronic messages (email, SMS, social-media DMs) need **consent**, **sender identification and contact information**, and an **unsubscribe mechanism**. The unsubscribe must be processed within 10 business days and remain valid for 60 days after the message.
- **Implied consent:**
  - an existing business relationship: a purchase or contract within 2 years, or an inquiry within 6 months
  - conspicuous publication of an email address without a "no CEMs" statement, where the message relates to the recipient's role
- **Express consent requests** must state the purpose and identify the requester (and anyone on whose behalf consent is sought). Pre-checked boxes are not valid.
- **s. 6(6)** exempts transactional messages from *consent*, but not from identification and unsubscribe requirements. Examples are quotes, transaction confirmations, warranty and account notices, and invoices.
- **Penalties and liability:**
  - Administrative monetary penalties up to C$1M per violation for individuals and C$10M for organizations.
  - Directors and officers can be personally liable, and there is vicarious liability for employees and agents.
  - s. 9 prohibits aiding, inducing or procuring a violation.
  - The private right of action was never brought into force.
- **CRTC "intermediary" liability:** the CRTC's 2018 *Datablocks/Sunlight Media* decision imposed liability on parties enabling violations in a malware case. [P; verify]

### Inferences
**CRM design requirements**
- Per-contact consent records: type (express or implied), source, timestamp, wording, and an implied-consent expiry date that auto-calculates the 2-year and 6-month windows.
- Separate "transactional" and "marketing" templates.
- Global unsubscribe and suppression for each client, synced across email, SMS and bot channels.
- Mandatory footer fields (legal name, address, unsubscribe link).
- Canadian-recipient detection, which applies CASL rules by default.
- Bounce and complaint monitoring.

**Vendor terms**
- An acceptable-use policy (AUP) prohibiting purchased lists.
- The right to suspend sending.
- Client warranty and indemnity for consent.

### Gaps
- The 2026 CRTC CASL enforcement record and the current CASL penalty figures were not re-fetched.
- The FTC Verkada order was not fetched directly.

## 7. Payments inside the ERP/AR module: PCI DSS, payment facilitation and money-transmission risk

### Takeaway
Keep card data out of the vendor's systems entirely. Use a PCI-validated processor's hosted fields or payment links and tokenized card-on-file, which shrinks the vendor's scope toward SAQ A. If the vendor does touch card data, it is a PCI "service provider" and must provide an Attestation of Compliance (AOC) and a responsibility matrix. The larger hidden risk is **touching the funds flow**. If the vendor receives client customers' payments into its own account before paying clients out, it may need state money-transmitter licenses (US) or registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act and FINTRAC (Canada). **Consult counsel before designing any funds flow.**

### Cited Findings
- **PCI DSS v4.0.1:** the "future-dated" requirements became mandatory March 31, 2025. Sensitive authentication data (CVV, full track data, PIN) may not be stored after authorization, including in call recordings and transcripts. — [PCI SSC document library](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/document_library/) [P]
- **Third-party service providers:** they must give customers written acknowledgement of their responsibility and a responsibility matrix (Req. 12.8 and 12.9), and customers must monitor their providers' PCI status. — [PCI DSS v4.0.1](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/document_library/) [P]
- **Telephone payments:** PCI SSC guidance recommends DTMF masking or pause-and-resume recording for card capture over the phone. This is relevant to AI voice payment capture. — [PCI SSC guidance](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/document_library/) [P]
- **Canada, Retail Payment Activities Act:**
  - Payment service providers that perform retail payment functions, such as holding end-user funds or initiating transfers, must register with the Bank of Canada. Registration opened in November 2024.
  - Risk-management and fund-safeguarding obligations took effect Sept 8, 2025.
  - Agents or mandataries of a registered PSP may be excluded.
  
  Source: [Bank of Canada RPAA](https://www.bankofcanada.ca/core-functions/retail-payments-supervision/) [P; verify]
- **US money transmission:** most states license money transmitters. "Agent of the payee" exemptions vary by state, and FinCEN MSB rules may also apply. — [CSBS](https://www.csbs.org/money-transmission-modernization-act) [P]

### Inferences
- **Safe architecture:**
  - Payments run on a processor such as Stripe Connect, Adyen or Moneris, with the processor acting as payment facilitator.
  - Funds settle directly to the client's merchant account.
  - The vendor stores only tokens.
  - Voice or chat bots send a secure payment link rather than collecting card numbers verbally.
  - Card numbers are redacted from transcripts and LLM prompts.

### Gaps
- The current RPAA exemption list and agent rules.
- State-by-state status of the agent-of-payee exemption.

## 8. HIPAA and health data (if a client niche is healthcare)

### Takeaway
For US covered entities such as clinics, dental and PT practices, the vendor hosting PHI in an ERP/CRM is a **business associate**:
- A BAA is mandatory **before** any PHI flows.
- The vendor is directly liable under the Security Rule.
- The vendor must have BAAs with sub-processors, including LLM, telephony, email and cloud providers.
- Breaches must be reported to the client without unreasonable delay and within 60 days.

Canadian health-information laws (Ontario PHIPA, Alberta HIA, and others) impose parallel "agent" or electronic-service-provider duties and sometimes residency expectations.

### Cited Findings
- BA obligations and BAA contents (45 C.F.R. §§ 164.502(e), 164.504(e)); BA breach notice to the covered entity within 60 days of discovery (§ 164.410); documentation kept 6 years (§ 164.316(b)(2)). — [HHS BA provisions](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/sample-business-associate-agreement-provisions/index.html) [P]
- **Civil money penalties** are tiered by culpability, with inflation-adjusted annual caps of about $2M or more per provision. — [HHS enforcement](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/compliance-enforcement/index.html) [P; verify figures]
- **PENDING:** HHS's January 2025 Security Rule NPRM. It would make encryption, MFA, asset inventories and annual audits explicit requirements. [P]
- **Ontario PHIPA** imposes duties on "health information network providers" and electronic service providers. — [Ontario PHIPA](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/04p03) [P]

### Inferences
- **Healthcare as a niche needs a separate product tier:**
  - BAA template
  - HIPAA-eligible cloud and LLM endpoints with zero-retention agreements
  - minimum-necessary data in bot prompts
  - audit logs
- **Patient-balance collections are consumer debt** (see section 9).
- **Appointment-reminder calls** use the TCPA healthcare exemption, which has limits.

### Gaps
- Status of the HIPAA Security NPRM in 2026.
- Provincial health-law residency specifics.

## 9. AR and collections features: FDCPA/Reg F vs B2B, state rules, Canadian provinces

### Takeaway
The FDCPA and Reg F cover only **consumer** debts collected by **third parties**. An SMB collecting its own B2B invoices in its own name is outside both. The traps:
1. **Wrong name.** A bot or dunning email using a name other than the creditor's triggers the FDCPA false-name exception and § 1692j.
2. **State first-party laws.** California's Rosenthal Act and Debt Collection Licensing Act (DCLA) and Massachusetts creditor rules reach creditors collecting their own consumer debts.
3. **California SB 1286.** Since July 1, 2025, Rosenthal also covers **commercial credit of $500k or less owed or guaranteed by a natural person**. This captures sole proprietors and personally guaranteed SMB customers.
4. **The vendor collecting in its own name** for many clients' consumer debts risks becoming a "debt collector" and needing state licenses.

### Cited Findings
- **FDCPA scope:**
  - "Debt" means consumer obligations for personal, family or household purposes (§ 1692a(5)). — [15 USC 1692a](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1692a) [P]
  - A "debt collector" has collection as its principal purpose or regularly collects debts owed to another. Creditors collecting in their own name are excluded, unless they use another name implying a third party (§ 1692a(6)).
  - § 1692j prohibits furnishing deceptive forms that imply a third party is involved. — [15 USC 1692j](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1692j) [P]
- **Reg F (12 C.F.R. Part 1006):** [P] — [eCFR 12 CFR 1006](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-X/part-1006)
  - 7-in-7 call-frequency presumption (§ 1006.14(b)(2))
  - no contact before 8am or after 9pm (§ 1006.6(b))
  - opt-out required in each email and text (§ 1006.6(e))
  - validation notice (§ 1006.34)
  - electronic delivery of disclosures (§ 1006.42)
  - 3-year record retention (§ 1006.100)
- **FDCPA damages:** actual damages, up to $1,000 statutory per individual, and for classes the lesser of $500k or 1% of net worth, plus attorney's fees. — [15 USC 1692k](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1692k) [P]
- **California SB 1286:** extends Rosenthal to "covered commercial credit" of $500,000 or less owed or guaranteed by natural persons, measured in aggregate. Applies to debts entered into, renewed, sold or assigned on or after July 1, 2025. Regulates substantive conduct only; no new licensing. — [Mayer Brown, June 2025](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/reminder-californias-new-commercial-debt-collection-protections-take-effect-july-1-2025); [Snell & Wilmer](https://www.swlaw.com/publication/californias-recent-expansion-of-the-rosenthal-fair-debt-collection-practices-act-to-commercial-debts/) [V]
- **California DCLA:** requires a DFPI license for persons, including first-party creditors, engaged in *consumer* debt collection in California. — [DFPI](https://dfpi.ca.gov/debt-collection/) [P]
- **Massachusetts:** 940 CMR 7.04 limits creditors to 2 calls per 7 days on consumer debt. — [940 CMR 7.00](https://www.mass.gov/regulations/940-CMR-700-debt-collection-regulations) [P]
- **Ontario Collection and Debt Settlement Services Act:** [V] — [Ontario CDSSA](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90c14)
  - Applies when the debtor or the agency is in Ontario.
  - Agencies must register.
  - Exclusions include "an isolated collection made by a person whose usual business is not collecting debts for other persons."
  - Conduct rules are in O. Reg. 74. — [O. Reg. 74](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/900074) [P]
- **BC:** Business Practices and Consumer Protection Act, Part 7, licensed by Consumer Protection BC. — [BPCPA](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/04002_00) [P]
- **Quebec:** Act respecting the collection of certain debts (CQLR c. R-2.2), with permits from the Office de la protection du consommateur. — [CQLR R-2.2](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/R-2.2) [P]

### Inferences
**ERP/AR design**
- Classify each receivable at ingestion: consumer vs business, personal guarantee or sole proprietor, and state or province.
- All dunning communications (bot, email, SMS) go out under the client's name.
- Apply frequency caps and calling windows by default (Reg F as a benchmark).
- Offer an opt-out in each electronic message.
- Escalate disputes, bankruptcies and attorney-represented debtors to a human workflow.
- Log every contact attempt.
- Never let the system or an LLM state amounts not in the ledger, or threaten action the client hasn't authorized.

**Vendor posture**
- The vendor provides software and the client is the collector.
- A white-label, vendor-branded "collections service" is a different regulated business. **Consult counsel** on FDCPA status and state licensing before offering it.
- CASL s. 6(6) covers invoice and reminder emails (no consent needed; identification and unsubscribe still required). The CAN-SPAM transactional exemption likewise covers pure reminders.

### Gaps
- Whether the Ontario, BC and Quebec statutes cover business-to-business debts was not confirmed.
- No list of US states that license *commercial* collection agencies was found.
- No case was found treating a SaaS collections-software vendor as an FDCPA debt collector.

## 10. Contracts and insurance: MSA, DPA, BAA, liability allocation, E&O and cyber

### Takeaway
Use an MSA, a DPA (with US-state, PIPEDA and Quebec terms), a BAA for health clients, and an AUP. Allocate liability by control:
- **The client** owns the lawful basis and consent, data accuracy, tax determinations, debt classification, and campaign decisions.
- **The vendor** owns security, platform controls, sub-processor management and its own data use.

Back this with tech E&O plus cyber insurance. Check the exclusions for TCPA, wiretap/BIPA and "unsolicited communications." **Consult counsel and an insurance broker.**

### Cited Findings
- CCPA service-provider terms are mandatory (Civ. Code § 1798.100(d); 11 CCR § 7051). — [CPPA](https://cppa.ca.gov/regulations/) [P]
- HIPAA BAA content is mandatory (§ 164.504(e)). — [HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/sample-business-associate-agreement-provisions/index.html) [P]
- PIPEDA accountability requires contractual protection for processors. — [OPC guidelines](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/airports-and-borders/gl_dab_090127/) [P]
- Quebec Law 25 requires written agreements for service providers and for transfers outside Quebec. — [Gowling WLG](https://gowlingwlg.com/en-ca/topics/canadian-privacy-laws-new-rules-for-a-new-era/quebec-law-25) [V]
- PCI DSS requires written acknowledgement and a responsibility matrix from service providers (Req. 12.8 and 12.9). — [PCI SSC](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/document_library/) [P]
- Blackbaud shows a hosting vendor bearing regulatory and attorney-general liability for clients' data. — [FTC Blackbaud](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/blackbaud-inc) [P]
- AI vendors are named directly in CIPA and TCPA suits (ConverseNow, Invoca, *Lowrey v. OpenAI*). — [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/news-insights/ai-call-monitoring-lawsuits-are-heating-up.html); [TCPAWorld](https://tcpaworld.com/2025/12/31/openai-liable-for-robocalls-texts-new-tcpa-complaint-claims-openai-and-twilio-are-liable-for-user-initiated-robotexts-violating-the-tcpa-and-this-could-change-everything/) [V]

### Inferences
These reflect practitioner norms, not specific sources.

**DPA must-haves**
- Processing on client instructions only.
- No training of shared models on client data, or an opt-in with de-identification.
- A sub-processor list with 30-day notice of changes.
- Data locations and a residency option.
- Security annex (SOC 2 scope).
- Breach notice to the client within 48–72 hours.
- Assistance with access, deletion and portability requests and with PIAs (Quebec).
- Deletion or return at termination, subject to retention locks requested by the client.
- Audit rights, satisfied by providing the SOC 2 report.
- Handling of lawful-access requests (CLOUD Act transparency).

**MSA terms**
- Client warranties: consent (CASL, CAN-SPAM, TCPA), right to collect, accuracy, tax configuration.
- Vendor warranties: SLA uptime, backups and RPO/RTO, calculation accuracy according to configuration.
- Mutual indemnities tied to control.
- Super-caps for data-breach, privacy and TCPA indemnities, often 2–5× annual fees or tied to insurance limits.
- Exclusion of consequential damages.
- An AUP banning purchased lists and illegal collection practices.
- The vendor's right to suspend sending.

**Insurance**
- Tech E&O (failure of the service, calculation errors).
- Cyber first-party coverage: incident response, business interruption, ransomware.
- Cyber third-party coverage: privacy liability, regulatory defense and fines where insurable, PCI fines and assessments, media liability.
- Confirm there are no TCPA, wiretap or BIPA exclusions, or buy a carve-back.
- Contractual-liability coverage for indemnities.
- Require clients that use outbound or collections features to carry their own coverage.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source was found on 2025–2026 cyber/E&O market exclusion trends or typical limits for SMB SaaS vendors.
- No model DPA was retrieved.
